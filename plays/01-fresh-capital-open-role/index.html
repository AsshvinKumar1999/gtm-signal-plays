Why it works

When a company raises money, the law makes it tell the SEC. When it posts a job, it tells everyone what it wants to fix next.

If both happen in the same week, the company has money and a plan. If the plan is the job your product helps with, you have a good reason to say hello.

How it works

  1. Read the list of companies that told the SEC they raised money this week.
  2. Throw out the investment funds. Keep the tech companies.
  3. Look for each company's jobs page.
  4. Keep the ones hiring the role your product helps.
  5. Check each one by hand. Then write a short note about their raise and their new hire.

Roles we found in this run

Real open jobs at the freshly funded tech companies from this run, pulled on 8 Nov 2026. Each company's jobs page was checked against its SEC filing. Jobs close fast, so some links may expire.

Is the data really free?

Yes. No sign-up and no API key. Here is what each source says about itself.

SEC EDGARraises

The SEC says Anyone can access and download this information for free. You add your name and email to each request, and stay under 10 requests a second. sec.gov →

Greenhousejobs

Greenhouse says authentication is not required for any GET endpoints. Reading jobs is open. greenhouse.io →

Leverjobs

Lever says published job postings are publicly viewable. A key is only needed to send applications, which we never do. lever docs →

Ashbyjobs

Ashby's own example is a plain request with no key. It returns the jobs a company already shows on its careers page. ashbyhq.com →

Where it can go wrong

Two companies can share a name.We might find the wrong jobs page. That is why step 5 checks each one by hand.
Some jobs pages are hidden.If a company posts jobs somewhere we don't look, it won't show up.
Some raises are late or missing.A company can file late, or not file at all. We can only see what was filed.
