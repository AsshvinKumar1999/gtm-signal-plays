Why it works

If you need a visa, the hardest part is finding companies that will sponsor one. Most job posts don't say.

But when a company wants to hire someone on an H-1B visa, it first has to file a wage form with the Department of Labor. Those forms are public. So are the SEC forms a company files when it raises money.

Put the two together with a live job post, and you get a short list: companies with new money, a history of sponsoring, and a job open right now.

How it works

  1. Run Play 01 to get tech companies that just raised money, and their open jobs.
  2. Read each company's tax ID from the same SEC filing.
  3. Look up that tax ID in the Department of Labor's H-1B filings.
  4. Keep the companies that filed before and are hiring now. Flag jobs like ones they sponsored.
  5. Claude reads your resume on your computer, ranks the jobs, and drafts a short note for each.

Is the data really free?

Yes. No sign-up and no API key. Both government sources ask you to put your name and email in each request.

SEC EDGARraises + tax ID

The SEC says Anyone can access and download this information for free. Stay under 10 requests a second. sec.gov →

Department of LaborH-1B filings

The Office of Foreign Labor Certification posts every quarter's H-1B wage filings as a free Excel file, about 250 MB. dol.gov →

Greenhouse, Lever, Ashbyopen jobs

Their public job board APIs need no key to read published jobs. Same sources as Play 01. see Play 01 →

Where it can go wrong

A filing is not a visa.The wage form is a first step. It shows the company was ready to sponsor, not that a visa was granted.
The past is not a promise.A company that sponsored before may not sponsor this job. Ask early and politely.
The data runs about 4 months behind.The newest Department of Labor filing in this run is from June 30, 2026. A company that started sponsoring after that won't show yet.
Names lie. Tax IDs don't.In the Labor data, 219 company names are shared by two or more different tax IDs. That's why we match on the tax ID. If a filing has no tax ID, we match by exact name, mark it "name-only", and you check it by hand.
"Similar job" is a hint, not a promise.We flag a job when its title shares two real words with a title they sponsored. Generic titles like "Member of Technical Staff" match a lot, so read the job before you count on it.
