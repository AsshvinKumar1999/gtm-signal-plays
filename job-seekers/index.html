JOB SEEKER KIT

Find companies with a dated reason to hire you

Before a company hires, it often has to file something public: a raise, a visa form, an FDA clearance, a government contract. Claude Code can read those records for free, check who is hiring, and help you write one honest note.

It will not get you a job by itself. It gets you a short list of companies worth your time, and a better first line than "I'd love to join your team".

The honest math

The record is the easy part. The job board check is where most companies drop out.

29 of 331funded tech companies had a Greenhouse, Lever or Ashby board
3 of 185FDA-cleared companies had one
2 of 76contract winners had one

No board does not mean no hiring. It means the script could not read their jobs, so you open their careers page yourself. Do that for your top 10. That is the part a script can't do for you.

How to use Claude Code for this

The idea comes from Jordan Crawford: start from what a company was forced to publish, not from what it wants you to see. Here is how that turns into a job search.

  1. Start from their situation, not a job title.Ask: "What did this company just have to file that means it needs people?" Then look for the job.
  2. Let code count. Let Claude judge.Ask Claude Code to write a small script that calls the free API and saves the results to a file. Don't ask it to "find funded startups that are hiring". It will guess.
  3. Match on IDs, not names.Use the tax ID, the contract ID or the FDA number. In the Labor data, 219 company names are shared by two or more different tax IDs.
  4. Open the top 5 yourself.Read the filing. Read the job. If the two don't fit together, drop it.
  5. Write one note that cites the record.Lead with their news, then one result of yours with a number, then one question. Keep it under 70 words.
  6. Keep your resume on your computer.No auto-applying, no LinkedIn bots, no logging in to anything. You send every note yourself.
  7. Run it again next week.Records are dated. A fresh list of 10 beats an old list of 500.

A note that cites the record

Fill in the brackets from the record and your resume. If you can't fill a bracket truthfully, don't send it.

Hi [first name], I saw [company] [what the record says, e.g. filed a Form D on (date)] ([source]). Teams in that spot usually need [the work this job does]. I [one result from your resume, with a number]. Is the [job title] role the right door for that, or is there someone better to ask?

Is the data really free?

SEC EDGARraises

The SEC says Anyone can access and download this information for free. Put your own name and email in each request. sec.gov →

USAspendingcontracts

Its docs say Endpoints do not currently require any authorization. usaspending.gov →

openFDAclearances

The docs say an API key is required. The key is free, and the docs also list limits for calls without one. Get the free key. open.fda.gov →

Greenhouse, Lever, Ashbyopen jobs

Greenhouse says authentication is not required for any GET endpoints. Lever and Ashby boards are public too. see Play 01 →

Where it can go wrong

A record is a reason, not a job.Money, a clearance or a contract means they will need people. It does not mean they need you, or this month.
The job may not be tied to the record.A company hiring after a contract win may be hiring for other work. The posts rarely say.
Some data runs late.The newest H-1B filing in my run was from June 30, 2026. Contract awards can post late too.
Small numbers are normal.One real company you can write to with a reason is a good run. Don't pad the list.

job-seeker-kit.prompt 
You are helping me find companies with a dated, public reason to hire, using the "compelled record" method from GTM Signal Plays by Asshvin Kumar.

Rules
- Use only free public data. No logins, no auto-applying, no LinkedIn automation, no scraping sites that block bots.
- My resume stays on my computer. Read it locally. Never upload it.
- Code counts, you judge. Every company and number must come from a file a script wrote. Never invent a company, a role or a fact about me.
- Match companies on IDs (tax ID, UEI, FDA number), not names, wherever the record has one.
- Put my own name and email in the User-Agent when a source asks for it (SEC, Department of Labor).

Step 0. Ask me 4 things:
  1. The path to my resume (PDF, DOCX or TXT).
  2. The roles I want. Turn them into a case-insensitive regex and show it to me.
  3. Where I can work, and whether I need visa sponsorship.
  4. My name and email for the User-Agent.

Step 1. Pick the record with me:
  - Startup roles: SEC Form D raises (Play 01).
  - Visa needed: Form D plus DOL H-1B filings (Play 06).
  - Medtech sales or clinical: FDA 510(k) clearances (openFDA; I'll get a free key).
  - Project, program or cleared roles: USAspending new contract awards.
  Explain your pick in one line.

Step 2. Get the script.
  For Play 01 or 06, download the scripts linked on their pages, read them, and tell me in 5 bullets what they do and which URLs they call.
  For FDA or contracts, write a small Python script (standard library only) that pulls the last 30 days, dedupes to one row per company, checks Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby boards for open jobs, and writes summary.json and accounts.csv.

Step 3. Run it. Show me summary.json in plain words, including how many companies had no jobs board.

Step 4. Rank and check.
  Keep jobs that match my roles and locations. Rank them by fit with my resume.
  For the top 5, open the source record and the job post, and tell me what you checked.
  List the top 10 companies with no jobs board, so I can open their careers pages myself.

Step 5. For my top 5, write notes.md:
  - Why this company, now: the record (with its date and link) and the open job.
  - The 2 lines of my resume that best match, quoted exactly.
  - A note under 70 words: their news first, one result of mine with a number, one question.

Step 6. Hand me shortlist.csv and notes.md. Don't send anything. I do that myself.
